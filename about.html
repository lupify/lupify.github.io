About me

I'm Lenny. I'm finishing a PhD in Applied Mathematics at UC Santa Cruz, in the Theoretical and Applied Complex Systems Lab, where I work on getting machine learning models of physical systems to behave like the physics they stand in for — staying stable over long rollouts, keeping the right energy at the right scales, and reconstructing what the observations never saw.

Before Santa Cruz I took an MS in Physics at San Francisco State and a BS in Physics at Stanford. The path between them ran through silicon photodetectors for a neutrino experiment at Stanford, real-time plasma control at Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory, aerial radiation signatures at Lawrence Berkeley, single-cell immunology at UCSF, and regional ocean emulation at Fujitsu Research of America. The common thread is large, messy, physical data, and the question of what a model has actually learned from it.

I like teaching this material as much as doing it. I've taught AI to middle and high school classrooms with Inspirit AI, tutored STEM one to one, and helped run volunteer science events.

Away from a screen I play volleyball and lacrosse, mountain bike, hike and camp, dance, tinker with robotics, play violin and sitar, read science fiction, and spend a great deal of time hunting for new music.
